      Rules for going back

        Gaps that matter most

        A Proposed ranking on the main values, from a study. Nothing here is decided: whether each gap belongs in the product is the owner’s call, after its own research.

          Decisions and their specs

          Everything the owner has decided, everything waiting for the owner, and everything still open with what settles it. Each links to the spec file and requirement IDs that carry it, the study it rests on and the parts it touches. A proposed or open item binds nothing until the owner agrees; “No spec file yet” means it waits for one. The architecture invariants and working rules hold over all of these, and each feature’s original words are in its feature note. The search box above finds decisions too.

          Ideas bank

          Good ideas seen in outside sources, kept for later study or building, each with its evidence and what comes next. Nothing here is decided or built, every measurement is UNEXECUTED, and “Measured” means by the source’s own authors on their tasks, never this project’s measurement. Choose a chip to narrow the list; the search box above searches it too.

          Codes

          A spec file’s code is the word that starts every ID in it: LOGIN-011 is requirement 11 of the login spec file, LOGIN-Q2 its second open question and LOGIN-V1 its first validation. This page shows the standard; the rule itself is in specs/README.md.

          The standard

          1. One English word, singular, in capitals, of 3 to 8 letters.
          2. Taken from the spec file’s name or title, so a reader can tell which file it is without a key.
          3. Never an abbreviation, an acronym or a blend.
          4. Used by no other spec file.
          5. Fixed once the spec file exists. Numbers are never renumbered or reused.

          About this map

          How to read it

            “Covered by” links to the records that cover a part; hover over one for its title. “Decisions” names the owner decisions (green), the proposed ones awaiting review (blue) and the ones still open (amber) that bear on the part. “Seen in” names the sources that describe it. A mark says what this repository records, not what is built. The map decides nothing and changes no requirement.

            Keeping it current

            1. project and repo name the project and its GitHub repository, as owner/name; without a repository, the page’s links open files in this folder. overview holds the overview’s labels. notice is a line under the title with a link (text, label, path), for as long as it is true. origin names the study the map comes from (label, path).
            2. The areas and parts come from the anatomy study, never from a preset list. Later studies add to them.
            3. Edit the data block at the top of this file, project-map-data. It is JSON, with one part per line; the page redraws every count, bar and diagram from it.
            4. A part has an id, name, meaning, status (Specified, Recorded, Gap or Out of plan), refs and seen, and may have a note and a priority (Gaps only).
            5. Each key in refs must be in the refs table, which holds its title and href; each code in seen must be in sources. A source has a title, a kind, read (how it was read) and may have segments: the pieces of its title, each with its text and, for a link, its url.
            6. New part: the next number in its area. IDs are never renumbered or reused. A part that no longer applies keeps its line, with "status": "Withdrawn: <date>, <reason>"; it leaves the counts.
            7. New area: the next A number, with place as [row, first column, end column] in the overview, or no place to sit with the areas around the product. Row 2 sits above the blue box and rows from 4 inside it; [4, 1, 5] spans its full width, and [4, 5, 6] sits outside it on the right. Add its links to edges: each names two of the areas, user and outside, and may add both or dashed.
            8. A decision in decisions has an id, title, status (Decided by the owner, Proposed for the owner’s review, or Open), when, specs (each spec key with its requirement IDs or § sections; empty while no spec file carries it) and parts; a decision with no parts shows under Project and process. It may have a study, a record and a recommended option, which Proposed needs. An Open one has settled_by (owner, review, measurement, study or other) and a note saying what it blocks or what settles it. A partly decided one has open_part. flag marks something the owner may want to reopen, and also names another label for it. When the owner agrees, set Decided and the owner’s time.
            9. An idea in ideas has an id (IDEA-NNN, never renumbered or reused), name, what, sources (S codes or a plain name), seen (a study’s key in refs, then the finding), evidence (Measured, Vendor’s test, Claim or Design only), gives (Success, Speed, Latency, Cost, Safety, Use), next (Owner, Measure, Study, Carry, Spec, Carried or Not for us), and settle, with specs and parts where they exist. A study of an outside source adds its good ideas here; when an idea moves, change its next and settle. idea_from names the sources for the filter.
            10. The Codes table is drawn from the spec files’ entries in refs, each keyed by its code. A new spec file’s code follows the standard and appears by itself.
            11. “How work moves” is drawn from stages, stage_rails, stage_now, loops and loop_rules. A loop has an id, from and to (stage IDs, later to earlier), seen (true once it has happened on this project), when, example and refs. The rule itself is in AGENTS.md.
            12. Add a line to changelog and set updated, with the project’s time.
            13. Run the project check, tools/check_project.py. It validates this data block: unique parts, known statuses, references that resolve, a link to every feature note and spec file, a row in decisions for every open question in a spec file, and each idea’s values, references, spec IDs and parts.
